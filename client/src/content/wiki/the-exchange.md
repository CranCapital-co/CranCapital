# The exchange

All goods trade on one global exchange. Every player in every city uses the same market.

## The order book

Each good has an order book. It lists the prices players want to buy at and sell at.

You can place two kinds of order:

- A market order fills straight away at the best prices available.
- A limit order names your price. Any part that can't fill now waits in the book.

When you place a buy order that waits, the money is set aside until it fills or you cancel. You can cancel an open order at any time.

Goods you buy go into your storage in the city you choose. You need free storage space there.

## Market traders

Market traders are run by the game. They always quote a price to buy and a price to sell, so you can always trade.

- They buy about 10% below a reference price.
- They sell about 10% above it.

That gap is where players can do better. Offer a price between the two and another player may take it.

## How prices move

Each good's reference price starts at its base price. Buying from traders pushes it up. Selling to them pushes it down.

Big orders move the price more, so the last units cost more than the first. Thinly traded goods such as electronics move faster than wheat.

Left alone, the reference price drifts slowly back towards the base price.

## The fee

The exchange charges a 1% fee on each trade. The seller pays it. Buyers pay no fee.

## Tips

- Check the trader prices before you place a limit order.
- Split a very large order into smaller ones to move the price less.
- Producers can auto-sell their output to market traders. It is simple, but you get the lower trader price.
