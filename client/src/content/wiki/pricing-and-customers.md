# Pricing and customers

Every sale starts with someone walking past. This page explains who walks past, and how many of them buy.

## Foot traffic

Each spot on the map has a foot-traffic score. It is higher near stations, sights and shops.

Foot traffic sets how many possible customers pass your business each hour. A quiet side street may bring a fraction of a busy square.

## Time of day

Customers follow each city's real local time. Every kind of business has its own rhythm.

| Business | Busiest hours (local time) | Weekend change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food cart | 12:00 to 14:00, with a smaller rush around 18:00 | about 15% busier |
| Café | 7:00 to 10:00 | no change |
| Restaurant | 12:00 to 14:00 and 18:00 to 21:00 | about 20% busier |
| Grocery | steady from 9:00 to 20:00, best around 17:00 to 19:00 | about 20% busier |
| Furniture and electronics stores | 10:00 to 18:00 | about 40% busier |

Almost nobody shops in the middle of the night. Paying staff through quiet hours rarely pays off.

## Fair prices

Every dish and product has a fair price. It is the same in every city.

Price at the fair price and a normal share of passers-by will buy. Price above it and fewer buy. Price below it and more buy.

| Your price | Customers who buy, compared with the fair price |
| --- | --- |
| 20% below fair | about 10% more |
| 10% below fair | about 5% more |
| At fair price | normal |
| 10% above fair | about 20% fewer |
| 25% above fair | about 42% fewer |
| 50% above fair | about 67% fewer |

The bonus for cheap prices stops at 40% more customers. A small markup can still earn more per sale, so test it.

## Variety

A bigger menu draws a few more people. Each dish you can actually serve adds about 5%, up to 15% extra with six dishes.

A dish only counts while you have its ingredients in stock.

## Competition

Businesses of the same kind split the crowd when they stand within 120 metres of each other. The closer the rival, the more customers you share.

One rival right next to you cuts your customers by about a third. A rival 60 metres away takes about a fifth.

Different kinds don't compete. A café doesn't take customers from a cart.

## Capacity

A business can only serve so many customers an hour. Extra demand is lost.

- Carts serve up to 40, 70 or 110 an hour, by level.
- Rooms depend on fit-out and staff. See [Shops and restaurants](#/wiki/shops-and-restaurants).

## Attractiveness

Decor in a room and higher levels make a business more attractive. An attractive business turns more passers-by into customers.

## A quick checklist when sales are low

1. Is the business open at this hour?
2. Are ingredients in stock in this city?
3. Is the price far above fair?
4. Did a rival open nearby?
5. Is the spot simply quiet? Try moving the cart.
